Research profile

Research, projects, and grants

I develop AI systems that remain dependable when data shift, unknown categories appear, inputs are corrupted or adversarial, and models must continue learning.

Research vision

My research asks how models can recognize unfamiliar or shifting data and behave reliably under adversarial or corrupted inputs. I study anomaly detection, open-world recognition, and learning over time, with a focus on computer vision and trustworthy machine learning.

I work at New Uzbekistan University and OIST, Japan. Earlier research appointments took me to IPM in Tehran, the University of Oulu, and Troyes. See my academic CV for background, or industry consulting for applied collaborations.

Research interests

The four themes connecting my theoretical work, evaluation methods, and practical AI systems.

Trustworthy machine learning

Reliable AI systems whose behavior can be evaluated beyond benchmark accuracy.

Anomaly and OOD detection

Recognizing unfamiliar, rare, shifted, or abnormal inputs.

Continual and lifelong learning

Learning over time while preserving useful knowledge and managing change.

Robustness, safety, and reliability

Studying adversarial, corrupted, backdoored, and failure-prone AI behavior.
