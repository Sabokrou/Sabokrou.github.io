What does Gini count?

Start with four training orders. A, B and C were on time. D was late. Pick a card, put it back, then pick again. A pair counts as different when one is late and the other is on time.

Pick A (on time), put it back, then pick D (late). Their outcomes differ. A followed by B gives the same outcome. You may draw the same card twice.

Gini = 6/16 = 0.375

With two labels, let p be the late fraction. The two ways to differ are late then on time, or on time then late. Their probabilities add to G = 2p(1 − p). Gini runs from 0 (all labels alike) to 0.5 (equal counts).

Check your understanding

At exactly 2.5 km in rain, which leaf do we reach?

The ≤ sign includes exactly 2.5. We take Yes and stop with A and B. Both arrived on time, so the estimate is 0/2 late.

Why did distance win at the root and rain win next?

The root compares questions using all eight orders. The next node compares questions using only the six longer trips. Rain has the largest Gini drop within those six.

Does 3 out of 3 late guarantee the next rainy delivery will be late?

No. The fraction describes the three training orders in this leaf. Check predictions on unseen deliveries to learn how well the rule generalizes.

Teacher prompt · Why stop at the mixed dry leaf?

This lesson limits each path to two questions. Two of the three dry, longer orders were on time, so the leaf predicts on time. A deeper tree could split again. In practice, choose depth using validation data.
